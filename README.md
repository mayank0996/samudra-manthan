# Samudra Manthan

My first project: a flood and tsunami protection model I built in Class 9, exhibited at the CBSE Regional Science Exhibition 2022-23 (theme Technology & Toy), where it competed toward Nationals.

## The idea

The name comes from the mythological churning of the ocean. India floods every year: Assam, the Bay of Bengal coast, cyclones pushing seawater into cities. The water has nowhere to go, so the idea was to give it somewhere to go. The concept came from my school teacher, Japan's underground flood-diversion works inspired it, and a senior who mentored me built it with me. I sketched the first version of the model on a train.

One honest note: Japan's tunnels handle inland river floods. My model was a classroom analogy for coastal surges, not a replica of that system.

## The build

The model is a box roughly 45 x 60 x 80 cm, in two levels like a bookshelf. On the top level, water flows in as high tides beside a modeled coastline and city. Water-level sensors watch the flow. When the water rises, a servo-operated gate opens and the water takes a tunnel instead of the city. On the way down it passes small hydro turbines, so the flood makes a little power, then collects in a storage tank that holds it until the surge passes and releases it slowly back to the sea. Mesh nets keep marine life out of the tunnel, and stones of the kind Mumbai uses to break wave pressure protect the tunnel mouth.

It cost around ₹10-20k to build, with ₹3,000 funded by the school. At the venue I kept it running by fixing leakage, turbine and connection issues by hand.

This project is where Arduino started for me. Before it, I was an electronics kid playing with batteries and motors. After it, I was a builder.

## What is documented and what is not

Demonstrated: the model was built and exhibited. Photographs from the exhibition (on-photo timestamp December 2022) show the team, the stall, the sensor wiring and a lit level indicator, and the stall's flowchart poster follows the same sequence: the sensor senses the level, the gate opens automatically, water runs to the well, the turbine produces electricity.

Not yet validated: the full sensing-to-gate-to-turbine chain never ran end to end under load. There is no measured power output and no full-scale performance claim. One bench trial remains open: measure flow, gate timing and turbine output on a real rig.

## What it means to me

Every project since, Trividya, Rudraksh and Atiras, is the same instinct with more engineering behind it: take a real disaster, study how another country answered it, and build a working model of a better answer.
